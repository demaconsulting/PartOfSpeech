# MASC (Written Genres)

License: CC-BY-3.0-US

Part-of-speech training data derived from the **Manually Annotated Sub-Corpus (MASC)** of the Open American National
Corpus: the Penn Treebank bracketing of its written genres, mapped to the 17 UPOS tags. MASC is distributed under the
[Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 United States License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/us/); the required
attribution and the statement of changes are in [ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md) and in
[THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](../../THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).

## Source

- **Corpus:** MASC, <https://anc.org/data/masc/>. The site states that MASC "can be downloaded by anyone for any purpose,
  under the conditions of the Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 United States License" and that the Open ANC from which it is
  drawn is "in the public domain or otherwise free of usage and redistribution restrictions".
- **Input file:** `penn-treebank.tgz` (2.9 MB), "Penn Treebank Syntax: syntax annotations for the entire 500K words of
  MASC in the original PTB (bracketed) format", release 2026-09-25,
  <https://github.com/nancyide/anc-website/releases/download/datasets-2026-09-25/penn-treebank.tgz>,
  SHA-256 `65ae26e4106cc576ad1291cf86a44048db7ff76fffa052658c45e8b1bb9f6230`, pinned in `tools/data/pins/masc.tsv`.
- **How produced:** `python tools/data/import_masc.py [--archive PATH_TO_penn-treebank.tgz]` reads the archive in place,
  skips the macOS resource-fork files (`._*`) and the spoken genre, maps every pre-terminal of every tree to UPOS with
  the table in [../CONVENTIONS.md](../CONVENTIONS.md) (a new function, `ptb_to_upos`; the tree walker is an adaptation
  of the one used for the earlier WordSense experiments, and the clitics stay split), keeps sentences of 3 to 70 tokens
  and writes the files below (including the source inventory `sources.tsv`). The re-run is bit-identical.

## Caveat: How the Part-of-Speech Layer Was Validated

The tags in `masc.tsv` are derived from the **Penn Treebank bracketing** of MASC, not from MASC's separate Penn
part-of-speech standoff layer. The history matters:

- The 2012 release notes (`README-MASC-3.0.0.txt`) say that the Penn POS tags of the standoff layer, "generated by a
  modified version of GATE's ANNIE Part-of-speech tagger, have not been fully validated", and that only the tags of the
  Penn Treebank-token and FrameNet-token layers are hand-validated, until the rest of MASC has been annotated for Penn
  Treebank syntax.
- The ANC site now describes the Penn Treebank syntax of the entire 500K words as released and states that all of MASC
  "includes manually validated annotations for sentence boundaries, token, lemma and POS; ... Penn Treebank syntax".
- We found no document that describes how the bracketing of the whole corpus was produced or validated. The statement
  that it is manually validated is the ANC's; it was not independently verified here.

The effect was therefore measured, not assumed. In the earlier experiments (13-class tag space of the WordSense
tagger) adding the mapped MASC trees to the open training split improved accuracy on UD English-EWT by 2.7 points and
reduced the harmful noun/verb confusions from 148 to 96. The conversion to UPOS adds its own rule-based noise (see the
known mapping noise in `../CONVENTIONS.md` and `mapping-audit.tsv`); phase 3 must re-measure on the open dev split
before keeping MASC in the final training mix. Source tags are kept in column 4 so any rule can be re-derived.

## Genres and License Basis

The corpus carries one license for all its documents, so the per-document table `sources.tsv` records the same class
(`CC BY 3.0 US`) for every row; its value is the inventory: a document that is not listed there cannot be in `masc.tsv`
(the unit tests and `tools/data/check_licenses.py` enforce this, and that the row counts equal the counts of `masc.tsv`).
Only the written genres are used. The license statement of the corpus (CC BY 3.0 US) covers all of MASC; no genre is
excluded for licensing reasons, and no Wikipedia, share-alike or non-commercial file name appears in the data. From the
file names, the technical genre consists of open-access journal articles (`journal.pbio.*`, `pmed.*`, `1468-*`, `1471-*`;
we did not check the article licenses one by one); the newspaper genre (53 documents, file names `nyt/*` and `apw_eng/*`,
29,463 tokens, 7.6 percent of the tokens) rests on the ANC's statement alone. If the project owner prefers to rely on more
than that statement, re-run the import with `--exclude-genre newspaper`.

## Leakage

No MASC sentence equals a sentence of an evaluation set, whatever its length (comparison of the lower-case alphanumerics):
open dev and test, gold v2, the shape probe, the STE challenge set, and UD English-EWT train, dev and test (fetched into
the git-ignored cache; only counts and MASC ids are recorded). A match of at least 25 key characters means shared source
text, so the whole document was removed: five email documents (`excluded-documents.tsv`), one overlapping UD English-EWT
dev and four overlapping UD English-EWT train. MASC and UD English-EWT share Enron email text. A shorter match is a
sentence that recurs in unrelated texts (`Thank you .`, `What should I do ?`), so only that sentence was dropped:
104 sentences with 392 tokens (`dropped-sentences.tsv`), each sentence of a treebank file being independent of the others.
